# MacroSilicon MS2130

Ids: 345f:2130 534d:2130 534d:0021 1e4e:7016
Vendors: 534d 345f
Link: 5000
Best: YUYV 1920x1080 @ 60
Hint: uncompressed YUYV at 1080p60 needs the 5000 Mbit/s link

Status: 1e4e:7016 tested 2026-09-06 PS5: picture, audio and sync at YUYV 1080p60
Status: 345f:2130 reported - common id for MS2130 boards
Status: 534d:2130 reported - MacroSilicon's own vendor id
Status: 534d:0021 reported - carried over from the earlier chip table; original source not recorded

The USB 3.0 successor to the MS2109, found in many cheap "4K" HDMI-to-USB
boxes (CNX Software covered one in 2022). "4K" is the input and loop-out;
capture is 1080p.

## Quirks

- Captures uncompressed YUYV 1080p60 only on a 5000 Mbit/s link; on USB 2 it falls back to MJPEG.
- Reported internal latency is about 6 to 11 frames (100 to 180 ms at 60 fps); no player setting reaches it. For twitchy games, play off the HDMI loop-out.
- The 3.5 mm mic jack is mixed into the captured audio; with nothing plugged in some units add hiss.
- No DV timings (`VIDIOC_G_DV_TIMINGS` is not supported), so signal lock cannot be queried; frames are the test.
- [1e4e:7016] Borrowed "Cubeternet" vendor id with no manufacturer or product string. MS2130 is inferred from uncompressed 1080p60 over USB 3, not read from the chip.
- [1e4e:7016] MJPEG at 1080p60 decodes in the Rust viewer: 2 undecodable frames in 2400, both at stream start (2026-10-16, #17). An earlier ffmpeg run reported "No JPEG data found in image". YUYV is still its best mode.
- [1e4e:7016] Offers 1920x1080 @ 60 only, in YUYV and MJPEG.
- [1e4e:7016] On a bus-powered USB 2 hub it reset-looped (276 resets an hour); on a USB 3 port, 2. Use a USB 3 port and the DC5V input.
- [1e4e:7016] Reports a USB serial, so it is pinned by `CARD_SERIAL`.
- [1e4e:7016] Can drop off the bus and re-enumerate when another USB device is plugged in.
- [1e4e:7016] Can get stuck sending broken frames on any PC: no YUYV frames at all (the driver drops them as incomplete) and MJPEG frames that decode as garbage. A full power cycle of the card clears it: its USB cable **and** its DC5V power out, then back (2026-10-18, the laptop); with DC5V connected, pulling the USB cable alone does not power it off. A computer restart, a device restart and reseated USB and HDMI cables did not clear it. Seen 2026-10-17 after many pull-and-replug cycles in a row, and 2026-10-18 after a single replug with the viewer reopening the card (#40).
- [1e4e:7016] The product: a blue aluminium box sold as "USB3.0 Video Capture HDMI 4K". One edge has `MIC | HDMI IN | LED | DC5V` (DC5V is USB-C power); the HDMI on the opposite edge is the loop-out, "HD Output" in the listing. The source goes into HDMI IN: plugged into the loop-out it captures nothing.
- [1e4e:7016] Its HDMI IN presents an EDID named "QCW MStar Demo 0x00000001" offering 1920x1080@60, so the source sees a 1080p60 display. A PC plugged into it gets a new monitor: on Hyprland it appeared as HDMI-A-2 and the desktop extended onto it. The name says nothing about the chip.
- [1e4e:7016] A real PS5 frame over YUYV measured luma 16 to 235, limited range, so LEVELS=auto is right for it.
- [1e4e:7016] On Windows (an Intel UHD 620 laptop, 2026-10-17, #32) an idle PC on mains loses about one frame a second below the viewer (22 to 70 gaps in 60 s at YUYV 1080p60); on battery, or with a CPU core or the GPU kept busy, 2 to 26. Processor idle disabled in the power plan cut it to 5 to 29. MMCSS "Capture" priority, a 1 ms timer, power throttling off, more outstanding reads, and capping the cores at C1 made no difference. Not yet known whether other cards do the same.
- [1e4e:7016] When the source sleeps it keeps streaming, a black frame with a small "No Signal V1.8" box, for about a minute, then sends no frames at all (seen with a PS5 in rest mode, 2026-10-13). The viewer's no-signal banner comes only after that.
